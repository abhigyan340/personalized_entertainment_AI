How the recommendation engine works

A hybrid ML pipeline that combines semantic embeddings, TF-IDF similarity, and metadata re-ranking to surface movies that genuinely match what you love.

When you enter a movie title, the engine first checks whether it exists in the TMDB 5000 training set. If it does, it uses pre-computed hybrid similarity matrices for fast, accurate retrieval. If the movie is outside the dataset, it fetches the live TMDB listing, generates a fresh embedding on the fly, and compares it against all 4,803 local movie vectors — so you always get results regardless of the title.

1

Retrieve

Two independent models encode every movie and compute pairwise similarity across the full 4,803-film corpus. Their outputs are combined into a single hybrid score that captures both surface-level keyword overlap and deeper semantic meaning.

Sentence Transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) TF-IDF vectorisation Cosine similarity Hybrid fusion
2

Re-rank

The top candidates from the retrieval step are scored on four structured metadata signals. This rewards movies that share concrete creative DNA with your input, not just similar descriptions.

Genres — 40% Keywords — 20% Cast — 20% Director — 20%

The final score blends both signals: 90% hybrid / semantic and 10% metadata, keeping recommendations content-driven while still honouring shared creators and genres.

3

Explain

Every recommendation card breaks down exactly why a movie was chosen — not just a score. You can see which genres, keywords, cast members, and director it shares with your input, alongside the raw AI match, hybrid similarity, and metadata percentages.

DATASET

TMDB 5000 Movie Dataset

The model is trained on 4,803 films sourced from The Movie Database. Each film is represented as a dense 384-dimensional embedding (Sentence Transformers) and a 5,000-feature TF-IDF vector built from genres, keywords, cast, director, and plot overview. Both representations are pre-computed and stored as NumPy arrays for sub-second retrieval at inference time.
